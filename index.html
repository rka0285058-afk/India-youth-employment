Graduate Jobs Gap Simulator

Move the levers to see how graduate hiring, enrolment growth and skills change unemployment and under-employment among young Indian graduates through 2035.

Starting point: 29.1% graduate-youth unemployment (ILO and IHD) with 11 million jobless graduates aged 20-29 (State of Working India), and 5.0 million graduate entrants against 2.8 million jobs a year. Modelled, not measured: no official series counts graduates working below their qualification, so that share is an assumption you can change.

How the model works

  • Each year, graduating cohorts (derived from higher-education enrolment, AISHE 2023-24) send a fixed share into the labour force. That share is set so 2024 matches the 5.0 million entrants reported by State of Working India.
  • Hiring is demand-driven and grows at your chosen rate from 2.8 million in 2024. The starting stocks are balanced so unemployment would stay flat without the changes you make.
  • The share of hires landing in graduate-level jobs equals employability (India Skills Report, trend only) times your "graduate-level share" setting. The rest take jobs below their qualification. Each year 10% age out of the 20-29 band, discouraged jobseekers leave at your set rate, and 5% of under-employed graduates move up.
  • The 2024 under-employed share (about 49%) is an upper-bound proxy, not an official statistic. Read trends and differences between scenarios, not exact levels.

Every input comes from a published source. Tier tells you how much weight it carries: A official statistics, B international or institutional research, C private survey (used only for the employability trend), M modelled or derived here. Monthly figures (current weekly status) are not comparable with annual usual-status figures.
